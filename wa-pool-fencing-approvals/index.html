WA pool fencing and approvals in Mandurah

In short: A private pool in Western Australia that is more than 300 mm deep needs a building permit and a compliant safety barrier. Local government inspects barriers at least every four years. Most pool fences no longer need their own building permit, except in defined cases.

A summary of official rules, with links to the source pages. It is not legal advice.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This page summarises official Western Australian information from Building and Energy so you know what to expect. It is not legal advice and doesn't replace your local government permit authority or a registered building surveyor. Follow the links and check with the council or your builder if you're unsure.

The short answer

Private swimming pools and spa pools in Western Australia that have a depth of water of more than 300 mm (30 cm) must have a child-resistant safety barrier. That includes portable and temporary pools, with some remote areas of the State excluded. Building and Energy also says a building permit must be in effect before you construct or install a private swimming pool.

Sources: Building and Energy, Swimming pool and spa safety barrier requirements; Building and Energy, Building permits for private swimming pools and pool fences (PDF).

Building permits for the pool and the fence

Anyone can apply for the building permit, typically the pool builder, the owner or the barrier installer. The person named as the builder on the permit is responsible for compliance. From 21 June 2024, most pool fences do not need their own building permit. A permit is still required for a fence that also acts as a fall-protection balustrade where someone could fall 1 m or more, a masonry fence over 0.75 m, a non-masonry fence over 1.8 m, or a fence in cyclonic wind region C or D, unless it is built under a relevant local law. Temporary installations of one month or less, and work in certain exempt regional areas, can also be exempt. Ask the City of Mandurah or your permit authority what applies to your block.

Source: Building and Energy, Rules for pools and spas (PDF).

Local council: Mandurah

Your local council is City of Mandurah. Check the council's planning information for your address (zoning, overlays, bush fire, flood or heritage mapping), as these can change what is allowed for a pool. Your builder or certifier normally does this check as part of the quote.

Some Mandurah properties are on canal estates or close to the estuary or the coast, and parts of the district are mapped as bushfire prone. Check the City of Mandurah planning information for flood, coastal and bushfire mapping, and any estate covenants, before you settle on a design.

Inspections after the pool is finished

On completion, the pool and its barrier must meet the applicable building standards. Local governments must inspect private swimming pool safety barriers at intervals of no more than four years, under the Building Regulations 2012, to confirm the barrier is still compliant.

Source: Building and Energy, Swimming pool and spa safety barrier requirements.

Builders, contracts and insurance

In Western Australia, the Home Building Contracts Act 1991 generally applies to fixed-price home building or associated work contracts between $7,500 and $500,000. For residential building work valued at more than $20,000, a registered builder must take out home indemnity insurance in the owner's name and give you the certificate before taking any payment or starting work. A registered building contractor is required when the work needs a building permit, is valued at $20,000 or more (or $50,000 or more for a Class 10a building such as a shed or garage), and is in the prescribed area of the State, unless an exemption applies. You can check a builder's registration with Building and Energy.

Sources: Building and Energy, Building or renovating your home; Building and Energy, Do I need to be a registered building contractor?; Building and Energy, Home indemnity insurance fact sheet.

Official pages checked 24 November 2026. Rules can change; check the linked sources or your local government before relying on this summary.
